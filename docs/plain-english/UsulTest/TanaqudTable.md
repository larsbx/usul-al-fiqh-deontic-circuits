# UsulTest.TanaqudTable in Plain English

Source: [`formal/lean/UsulTest/TanaqudTable.lean`](../../../formal/lean/UsulTest/TanaqudTable.lean)

For each of fifteen pairs of clashing qawāʿid (legal maxims), the file prints the full truth table: what the first rule says, what the second says, and what their reconciliation says, case by case. It is generated by `python -m usul_corpus signature` from `corpus/tanaqud/*.json` and must not be edited by hand.

## 1. What a row checks

`Usul.Tanaqud` models each tanāquḍ (internal contradiction) as a `Pair`. Two rules, each read without its qayd (restricting condition), rule differently on some case. The qayd splits the cases between them. The resolution is jamʿ (reconciliation): each rule governs on its own side.

Each pair `tN` gets three lines per case:

- **`tN.first c`**: the first rule's ruling on case `c`.
- **`tN.second c`**: the second rule's ruling.
- **`tN.jam c`**: the reconciled ruling. It equals `first` on the first rule's side and `second` on the other.

There are 138 guards. Each pair is checked on every possible case, so the table shows exactly where the two rules clash and which one the qayd hands each case to.

## 2. The fifteen pairs

T1–T10 are inside al-Saʿdī's bank of qawāʿid. T11–T15 carry the method to Ibn Rajab, inside his book and against al-Saʿdī.

| Pair | Clash | Reconciled ruling (`jam`) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `t1` (Q13 ⟂ Q27) | Liability for itlāf (destruction) by someone forgetful or ignorant. | A human's right: liable in every mental state. God's right: liable only when deliberate. |
| `t2` (Q20 ⟂ Q25) | Property whose entitled owner is unknown. | Known competing claimants: cast lots. No one identifiable: public interest. |
| `t3` (Q31 ⟂ Q41) | Two acts of worship of one kind, such as two kaffārāt. | Same purpose: one performance. Different purposes: two. |
| `t4` (Q55 ⟂ Q57) | A confessor who says he meant otherwise. | Bound by his words only when another's right is attached and claimed. |
| `t5` (Q17 ⟂ Q39) | Acting before the time. | The early act stands only after its cause has occurred. |
| `t6` (Q4 ⟂ Q40) | Able to do only part of a command. | A part is obligatory if it can be done and is not a pure means (wasīla maḥḍa). |
| `t7` (Q21 ⟂ Q50) | Gharar (uncertainty) in what follows a sale, like a house's foundations. | Forbidden only when the uncertain item does not follow a known whole. |
| `t8` (Q11 ⟂ Q52) | A presumption against a contrary indicator. | The presumption stands against wahm (fancy) and shakk (doubt); it yields to ẓann (probability) and qaṭʿ (certainty). |
| `t9` (Q12 ⟂ Q45) | Whose consent is needed. | Concluding an exchange needs the other's consent; a unilateral right does not. |
| `t10` (Q10 ⟂ Q26) | A trustee who claims the thing perished. | Held in trust: his oath (yamīn) suffices. Otherwise: he needs proof (bayyina). |
| `t11` (Ibn Rajab 66 ⟂ 6) | An act done on a belief later shown wrong. | Stands if a valid basis exists in fact; otherwise stands only if no other's right is attached and he was not negligent. |
| `t12` (al-Saʿdī Q28 ⟂ Ibn Rajab 7) | The original becomes available mid-substitute. | A substitute taken from necessity: switch. A general concession: keep going. |
| `t13` (Ibn Rajab 32 ⟂ 33) | Excepting a benefit for an uncertain period. | Valid if the law creates the exception or the transfer already bears that uncertainty. |
| `t14` (Ibn Rajab 1 ⟂ al-Saʿdī Q9) | Running water: one body or jirya by jirya (flow by flow)? | In an oath: one body, by custom. Otherwise (ṭahāra): flow by flow. |
| `t15` (Ibn Rajab 14 ⟂ 15) | A breach of purity certain in one of two people. | Taken together in one ruling: the breach attaches. Each alone keeps his presumption. |

## 3. Representative rows

- `t1.jam (.allah, .forgetful) == false`: a forgetful muḥrim who kills game owes nothing, although Q13 alone (`t1.first`) says he does.
- `t8.jam .shakk == true` but `t8.jam .zann == false`: doubt does not dislodge a presumption; probability does.
- `t11` is the largest table (24 guards), over three yes/no facts.

## In one line

Every apparent contradiction between two qawāʿid is tabulated case by case, and the table shows that the author's own qayd splits the cases so both rules are kept.
