# Joint agency: candidate source profile

Reviewed: 2026-11-06. Status: **candidate comparative model**, with no promotion
of a corpus record or claim to accepted normative data.

## Compact profile

The pilot studies one ordinary, revocable mandate from a living, capable
principal to two agents, A and B, for one fixed task. A valid initial joint
appointment known to both agents and the transaction's remaining conditions
are assumptions. A shared solo-permission
flag means explicit permission for **each** agent to act separately; its absence
means neither has that permission. The model keeps two reported revocation
positions separate: termination on the principal's revocation, and termination
for an agent upon that agent's knowledge of revocation. A joint act requires
effective authority of both agents. Applying the latter requirement when only
one agent knows is an **explicit candidate composition**, not a mixed-knowledge
ruling quoted from the sources. Each agent may also withdraw personally.
One accepted execution creates a persistent receipt and prevents another
acceptance for this fixed task, an application restriction rather than a general
rule about agency. Knowledge is a supplied fact. Subdelegation, partial
revocation by the principal, reappointment, death, incapacity, third-party
rights, and complete transaction validity are outside this pilot. Sources and
the limits of each mapping appear below.

The web entry numbers are navigation identifiers, not masala numbers. In
particular, **Q62 concerns revocation before knowledge; Q69 concerns a specified
task and substitution of another performer**. They are not interchangeable.

K1's brief textual anchor is “لم يجز لأحدهما الانفراد بالتصرف”. M2's second
reported position includes “لا ينعزل قبل علمه”. These anchors identify the
discussions; the surrounding passages determine their scope. The discussion of
Abu Hanifa near the end of M2 must not be silently attributed to the Hanbali
positions.

## Source-to-model mapping

| Model element | Source support or modeling status |
|---|---|
| Exactly one principal and two agents | Deliberate restriction to the two-agent case in K1/M1. The `appoint` event abstracts an initial appointment assumed valid and known to both agents. |
| Joint action is the default | K1/M1. The model does not infer independent authority from an agent's absence, silence, or inability to participate. |
| Shared solo-permission Boolean | Restriction to the **symmetric** case: true grants separate authority to both agents; false grants it to neither. K1 also permits an asymmetric grant to one specified agent, which this Boolean cannot express. |
| Actual revocation event | The principal revokes the whole modeled mandate. Revocation of only one appointment and later reinstatement are excluded. |
| Individual withdrawal events | M2 permits an agent's own withdrawal. K1/M1 expressly prevent the remaining joint-only agent from acting alone when the other resigns. `withdrawA` and `withdrawB` permanently remove that agent's effective authority in both profiles. |
| Other agent after withdrawal | With explicit independent permission, the other agent may still act alone if otherwise effective. This is the model's composition of personal withdrawal with the other's separately granted authority; the cited resignation sentence itself concerns the joint-only case. |
| Separate knowledge events for A and B | An abstraction of each agent's knowledge of the actual revocation. A transport acknowledgment or queued notification does not establish that fact by itself. |
| Immediate-revocation profile | M2's reading under which the agent is removed whether informed or uninformed. |
| Knowledge-dependent profile | M2's other reported position, preserved as a separate named alternative. R62 corroborates the existence of the dispute. |
| Both agents need effective authority for an expressly joint act | **Candidate composition** of joint agency and per-agent revocation. The cited passages do not expressly decide the combined mixed-knowledge fact pattern. |
| One persistent accepted receipt | Application restriction to one accepted execution of the fixed task. It is not sourced as a general rule that every mandate ends after its first act, or as evidence of real-world settlement. |
| No subdelegation operation | Scope exclusion. R69 is not encoded as a universal prohibition and solo permission is not permission to appoint a substitute. |

The profile names identify modeled alternatives; they do not certify either as
the unique, preferred, or universally applicable Hanbali position. The chosen
profile and independent-permission flag remain fixed throughout a modeled run.

## Explicit composition for mixed knowledge

The [Lean model](../../formal/lean/Usul/Agency.lean) uses `appointed` for the
initial grant, `revoked` for the principal's actual revocation, `knownA/knownB`
for knowledge of revocation, and `withdrawnA/withdrawnB` for personal withdrawal.
Below, `known(i)` and `withdrawn(i)` abbreviate the corresponding agent's fields;
`independent` is the symmetric solo-permission flag. The executable guards are:

```text
effective(immediate, i) = appointed and not withdrawn(i) and not revoked
effective(onKnowledge, i) = appointed and not withdrawn(i) and not known(i)
pending = (accepted = none)

executeJointEnabled = pending and effective(profile, A) and effective(profile, B)
executeSoloEnabled(i) = pending and independent and effective(profile, i)
```

These formulas concern the fixed in-scope execution events. The explicitly
outside-scope execution events are disabled. The initial state has no
appointment or receipt, and both agents' knowledge and withdrawal fields are
false. A learning event records knowledge of an actual revocation, so reachable
states satisfy `known(i) implies revoked`. On those states, `not known(i)` is
equivalent to `not (revoked and known(i))`; that equivalence must not be claimed
for arbitrary raw states. The exhaustive wire comparison includes raw states
that violate the causal invariant, but their inclusion establishes software
agreement, not that they represent coherent legal histories. Knowledge,
withdrawal, revocation, and appointment are monotone during this single
mandate; there is no reappointment or reset operation.

For reachable states with an appointment, **neither agent withdrawn, and no
existing receipt**, both profiles enable joint execution before revocation and
enable solo execution exactly when independent permission is present. After
revocation the immediate profile enables neither. Under the same withdrawal
and receipt assumptions, the knowledge-dependent profile after revocation uses
this explicit composition:

| A knows | B knows | Joint execution | A alone | B alone |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| No | No | Authorized | Only with solo permission | Only with solo permission |
| Yes | No | Not authorized | Not authorized | Only with solo permission |
| No | Yes | Not authorized | Only with solo permission | Not authorized |
| Yes | Yes | Not authorized | Not authorized | Not authorized |

In particular, the model neither waits for **both** agents to know before
removing the knowing agent's authority, nor lets an uninformed agent bypass a
joint-action condition. A mixed-knowledge joint attempt is not silently
reclassified as a valid solo act. If the uninformed agent has separate
permission, that agent's solo execution is represented as a distinct action.
This treatment is a reviewable formal choice pending review of the combined
case; it is not presented as an explicit ruling from K1, M2, or Ibn Rajab.

Withdrawal adds a separate condition to every row: a withdrawn agent cannot
participate under either profile, and a joint execution requires neither agent
to have withdrawn. If A withdraws, B can execute alone only when independent
permission is present, B remains effective, and the receipt is still empty;
the corresponding statement holds with A and B exchanged. Once a receipt
exists, every execution is disabled irrespective of either agent's current
authority. This last condition is the application acceptance policy.

## Meaning of an execution result

An accepted execution means that the modeled agency conditions and the
single-use acceptance condition hold **at the chosen execution event**. An
empty receipt is an application precondition, separate from legal authority.
The result does not establish all the conditions of a sale, payment, release,
or other underlying transaction. Missing authority also does not by itself
decide personal blame, compensation, subsequent ratification, or the rights of
another party.

Execution is atomic at this level. The two agents participate in the same
modeled joint act; two stored approvals from different times are not
automatically equivalent to that act. A workflow with signatures, delivery
queues, or separate approval and settlement steps needs its own refinement and
an identified moment at which authority is evaluated. Revocation versus
execution can be explored through the two event orders without treating either
order as a ruling about real-world simultaneity.

A later revocation, knowledge, or withdrawal event does not retroactively
change the model's classification of an earlier execution. The accepted actor
set is retained as the receipt; a proof-side run can supply the facts at
execution. The receipt alone is not a full audit log, evidence of physical
performance, or a real-world settlement certificate. The transition relation
has no successor for a disabled attempt, so its runs describe enabled events,
not a log of every rejected request.

## Deliberate exclusions and remaining review

The pilot includes individual resignation and one accepted execution of its
fixed task. It has no events for death, incapacity, a third party's attached
right, expiry, asymmetric solo permission, delegation to another agent, partial
revocation by the principal, or a new mandate. Its one-shot receipt does not
formalize general rules about which acts exhaust a mandate or prove that the
underlying task has been completed in the world. Excluding a case is not a
ruling that it has no legal effect. There are no claims here about judicial or
guardian authority, collateral-sale mandates, or present-day jurisdictional
law.

The remaining doctrinal gap is the **combined mixed-knowledge joint-agency
case**. The source passages independently support the joint-agent rule and the
two revocation positions; the conjunction used here is proposed for review.
The remaining application gap is how real evidence establishes appointment,
the scope of permission, actual revocation, knowledge, and the execution
event. Formal checks establish consequences of the selected definitions and
assumptions; they do not establish those facts or confer scholarly approval.
